# Selling to souvenir shops

Researched 2026-09-24 for Gus: how the souvenir trade works, how to get in, what to say, how to negotiate, how restocking works.

## How the business works

- Shops buy at wholesale and sell at retail. The standard is keystone: wholesale is half of retail. Our line sheet already says $12.50 wholesale, $24.99 retail.
- Three ways a shop takes a product:
  1. **Wholesale.** The shop buys the decks up front. The shop carries the risk. Best for Gus.
  2. **Consignment.** The shop pays only when a deck sells, usually 60/40 or 50/50 to the maker. The maker carries the risk and has to count stock and chase payment. Use it only to get into a shop that says no to wholesale, and get the terms in writing.
  3. **Sale or return (middle ground).** The shop buys wholesale, but Gus promises to swap unsold decks for another design. The shop feels safe and Gus still gets paid.
- Opening orders for small brands are usually $150 to $300. Reorders are smaller, often $75 to $100. Our opening order (12 decks, $150) fits.
- First order is normally paid on delivery. Net 30 (pay within 30 days) comes after a shop has paid once. Our line sheet already says this.
- Sales tax: shops give the maker a copy of their Florida Annual Resale Certificate and pay no sales tax on wholesale orders. Keep a copy of each shop's certificate on file for the year. Gus's business needs to be registered with the Florida Department of Revenue to sell wholesale.

## When to pitch

- Miami high season runs about December to April. Shops fill shelves for it in October and November. Late September into October is the right time to start.
- Walk in on a weekday morning, not on a weekend or when a cruise ship or tour bus is in.

## Where to pitch, in order

1. Shops where the deck is obviously local: Little Havana (Calle Ocho shops, cigar shops, the visitor center near Domino Park), South Beach (Art Deco Welcome Center shop, Ocean Drive and Collins souvenir shops), Wynwood (shops near Wynwood Walls, where Gus works).
2. Museum stores: HistoryMiami, Vizcaya, Pérez Art Museum, Frost Science. They want local products but often ask for a UPC barcode, insurance and net 30, and they buy months ahead. Pitch by email to the store buyer.
3. Hotel gift shops and concierge shops on Miami Beach and Brickell.
4. Later: Miami International Airport shops. These are run by large concession companies (Hudson, Paradies). They need barcodes, volume and paperwork. Not a first step.
5. Trade show, when there is money for it: Surf Expo in Orlando has a coastal gift and souvenir section (January and September, trade only).

## What to bring

- 2 or 3 finished decks as samples (needs the MPC samples first). One opened so the owner can hold the cards.
- The one-page line sheet (callenueve.com/wholesale PDF): photo, prices, case size, opening order, terms, contact.
- A small counter display: a tray that holds 6 decks with a header card ("Play dominoes anywhere"). A display makes a yes easier because the shop does not have to find shelf space.
- An order form or a Faire Direct link on a card (see below).

## What to say (about 60 seconds)

Ask for the owner or buyer first. If they are not in, leave a sample and the line sheet, get a name and the best day to come back.

> "Hi, I'm Gus. I make Calle Nueve, a Cuban domino game in a deck of cards. It's made here in Miami. Every card is one domino tile, so it plays exactly like dominoes, and it fits in a pocket. Visitors see dominoes at Domino Park and can't take a set home in a suitcase. This they can.
>
> It sells for $24.99 and you buy it at $12.50. The opening order is 12 decks, and they come with a counter display that holds six. If a design doesn't move in 60 days, I'll swap it for one that does.
>
> Can I leave this one with you? I'll come back Thursday to see what you think."

Why it works: it says what it is in one line, why their customer wants it, the money for the shop, and it takes the risk away.

## How to negotiate

- Lead with wholesale. Offer the swap promise before offering consignment.
- If the shop wants a lower price, keep the price and give something else: a free display, a free tester deck the staff can open, or 13 decks for the price of 12 on the opening order. Keep one wholesale price for every shop in the same area; shop owners talk.
- If they want fewer than 12, let them start with one case of 6 at the same price. A small yes is better than a no.
- If they will only do consignment: 60 percent to Gus, a written agreement (price, split, payment date each month, who pays for damaged or stolen decks), and a maximum of 12 decks out at one time.
- Ask for the display spot by the register. Small, cheap-to-carry items sell as impulse buys there.

## Restocking (where the money is)

- Visit every 2 to 4 weeks at first. Count what is left, tidy the display, write the reorder on the spot, and take payment for the last order if it is due.
- Keep a simple list per shop: date, decks delivered, decks left, reorder, paid.
- Faire (online wholesale marketplace): a shop orders through Faire, Faire pays Gus and gives the shop net 60. On shops Gus brings himself through his own Faire Direct link, Faire takes 0 percent. On shops that find him on Faire, Faire takes 15 percent plus $10 on the first order. Useful for easy reorders and for shops outside Miami.

## Money check before the first pitch

At $12.50 wholesale, the deck plus box must cost about $7 or less for this to be worth it (from `RESEARCH_souvenir_line.md`). Get the MPC sample cost and Mr. Playing Card's quote first. If the cost is higher, a bulk order (500 or more) or a local printer is needed before wholesale makes sense.
